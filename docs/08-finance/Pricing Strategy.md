## 1. Purpose

This document defines the pricing approach FixNest should use to balance customer trust, technician earnings, operational sustainability, and business profitability.

## 2. Pricing Objectives

FixNest pricing should:

- Build trust through transparency.
- Reduce negotiation friction between customers and technicians.
- Provide fair income for technicians.
- Protect FixNest gross margin.
- Support repeat usage and long-term customer relationships.
- Remain flexible for different cities, service areas, and job complexity.

## 3. Pricing Principles

- Customers should understand expected charges before work begins.
- Standard jobs should have published starting prices or fixed prices.
- Non-standard jobs should use inspection-based quotations.
- Material costs should be separated from labor where practical.
- Emergency, night, or peak-time services may carry a premium.
- Prices should be reviewed regularly using market data, technician feedback, and customer feedback.

## 4. Price Components

A FixNest job may include:

1. **Visit Charge**: Fee for technician travel and inspection.
2. **Diagnostic Charge**: Fee for identifying the issue when detailed testing is required.
3. **Labor Charge**: Fee for performing the repair, installation, or service.
4. **Material Charge**: Cost of parts, consumables, or equipment.
5. **Platform Service Fee**: FixNest margin or commission embedded in the service price or shown separately.
6. **Emergency Premium**: Extra charge for urgent, after-hours, or high-priority dispatch.
7. **Warranty or Guarantee Cost**: Included in eligible service pricing where applicable.

## 5. Pricing Models

### 5.1 Fixed Price

Use fixed pricing for simple repeatable services with predictable scope, such as basic fan installation, standard AC general service, or tap replacement where parts are separate.

### 5.2 Starting Price

Use starting prices when the work category is predictable but actual complexity may vary, such as leakage repair, wiring fault diagnosis, or AC cooling diagnosis.

### 5.3 Inspection-Based Quote

Use inspection-based quotations for complex, hidden, high-risk, or material-heavy jobs such as concealed pipe leakage, major electrical panel repair, renovation work, or appliance part replacement.

### 5.4 Package Pricing

Use packages for preventive maintenance and recurring services, such as AC seasonal service bundles, home inspection packages, or Annual Maintenance Contracts.

### 5.5 Subscription Pricing

Use monthly or annual membership pricing for future homeowner plans that include priority support, discounted visits, maintenance reminders, and selected free inspections.

## 6. Launch Pricing Categories

### 6.1 Electrician

Example price structure:

- Visit or inspection charge.
- Fixed charges for simple installations.
- Starting price for wiring faults.
- Custom quote for panel work, rewiring, or major fault repair.

### 6.2 Plumber

Example price structure:

- Visit or inspection charge.
- Fixed charges for simple fixture replacement.
- Starting price for leakage or blockage.
- Custom quote for concealed leakage, pipe replacement, or water motor work.

### 6.3 AC Service

Example price structure:

- Fixed price for standard general service.
- Diagnostic charge for cooling issues.
- Custom quote for gas leakage, part replacement, compressor issues, or installation work.

## 7. Commission and Margin Approach

FixNest may use one or more of these approaches:

- Commission percentage from technician labor earnings.
- Markup on standardized service price.
- Platform service fee paid by customer.
- Margin on supplied materials or products.
- Subscription revenue from premium plans.

For launch, FixNest should prioritize simple commission or margin rules that are easy to explain and reconcile.

## 8. Discount Strategy

Discounts should be controlled and tracked. Recommended discount types:

- First-time customer discount.
- Referral discount.
- Seasonal AC campaign discount.
- Bundle discount for multiple services at one address.
- Loyalty discount for repeat customers.

Discounts should not create customer expectations that FixNest is a low-quality bargain service. The brand should compete on trust, reliability, and transparency.

## 9. Emergency Pricing

Emergency jobs may include a premium for:

- Same-hour dispatch.
- Night service.
- Public holiday service.
- Severe leakage, electrical safety, or urgent system failure.

Emergency pricing must be communicated before dispatch whenever possible.

## 10. Material Pricing

Material charges should follow these rules:

- Customer should know whether materials are included or excluded.
- Technician should share material requirement before purchase where practical.
- Receipts should be collected for significant purchases.
- FixNest may develop approved supplier rates in later phases.
- Material warranties should be separated from labor warranties.

## 11. Price Governance

FixNest should maintain a pricing master sheet with:

- Service category.
- Service name.
- City or area.
- Fixed price or starting price.
- Technician payout.
- FixNest margin.
- Warranty eligibility.
- Last updated date.
- Approval owner.

## 12. Review Cadence

Pricing should be reviewed:

- Monthly during launch.
- Quarterly after stable operations.
- Immediately after major market, fuel, supplier, or wage changes.

## 13. Success Metrics

Pricing success should be measured by:

- Quote approval rate.
- Customer complaint rate about pricing.
- Average order value.
- Gross margin per job.
- Technician earnings per job.
- Repeat customer rate.
- Discount dependency.
- Cancellation rate after quote.
