# FixNest Financial Plan v1.0

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the initial financial planning framework for FixNest. It supports launch budgeting, revenue planning, cost control, unit economics, and future fundraising preparation.

## 2. Financial Objectives

FixNest should aim to:

- Reach repeatable positive gross margin per completed job.
- Control customer acquisition cost.
- Build a reliable technician payout and commission model.
- Invest in trust, quality, and customer support without overspending too early.
- Expand only when service quality and unit economics are stable.

## 3. Revenue Streams

### 3.1 Launch Revenue

- Service commission or margin on electrician jobs.
- Service commission or margin on plumbing jobs.
- Service commission or margin on AC service jobs.
- Visit, diagnostic, or emergency service fees where applicable.

### 3.2 Growth Revenue

- Additional service categories.
- Material or parts margin.
- Annual Maintenance Contracts.
- Premium homeowner memberships.
- Priority support fees.
- Service bundles.

### 3.3 Platform Revenue

- Product marketplace margin.
- Supplier partnerships.
- Featured provider plans.
- Extended warranties.
- Home inspection packages.
- Renovation project management fees.

## 4. Cost Structure

### 4.1 Variable Costs

- Technician payouts.
- Payment processing charges.
- Discounts and refunds.
- Customer support cost per booking.
- Warranty rework cost.
- Materials where FixNest supplies items.

### 4.2 Fixed and Semi-Fixed Costs

- Operations staff.
- Customer support staff.
- Technology tools.
- Website hosting and software subscriptions.
- Marketing retainers or content production.
- Office or administrative expenses.
- Legal, accounting, and compliance.

### 4.3 Growth Costs

- Paid advertising.
- Referral incentives.
- Technician onboarding and training.
- Service area expansion.
- Partnership development.
- Product and platform development.

## 5. Unit Economics Framework

For each completed job, FixNest should calculate:

- Customer price.
- Technician payout.
- Material cost paid by FixNest if applicable.
- Payment fee.
- Discount.
- Refund or warranty provision.
- Gross profit.
- Gross margin percentage.
- Customer acquisition cost allocation.
- Contribution margin.

A job is financially healthy when gross profit covers direct operating effort and contributes to marketing, technology, and overhead.

## 6. Key Financial Metrics

- Gross bookings value.
- Net revenue.
- Gross profit.
- Gross margin percentage.
- Average order value.
- Completed jobs.
- Cancellation rate.
- Refund rate.
- Warranty rework cost.
- Customer acquisition cost.
- Lifetime value.
- Repeat customer rate.
- Technician payout ratio.
- Monthly burn rate.
- Cash runway.

## 7. Launch Budget Categories

FixNest should prepare budgets for:

- Website and booking setup.
- Brand identity and content.
- Customer support tools.
- Technician verification and onboarding.
- Initial marketing campaigns.
- Legal and accounting setup.
- Operating reserve.
- Emergency refund or service recovery reserve.

## 8. Financial Assumptions to Track

Initial assumptions should be documented and updated monthly:

- Average service price by category.
- Average technician payout by category.
- Expected jobs per day.
- Completion rate.
- Repeat customer rate.
- Average discount per job.
- Average customer acquisition cost.
- Complaint and rework rate.
- Support workload per booking.

## 9. Three-Phase Financial Plan

### Phase 1: Launch

Primary goal is proof of demand and service reliability. Spend should focus on technician supply, booking operations, customer support, and limited high-intent marketing.

### Phase 2: Growth

Primary goal is repeatable acquisition and expanded service coverage. Spend should increase only in channels with acceptable cost per completed booking.

### Phase 3: Platform

Primary goal is higher customer lifetime value through marketplace, Annual Maintenance Contracts, subscriptions, and AI-enabled operations.

## 10. Cash Management Rules

- Maintain a minimum operating reserve.
- Reconcile cash collections daily.
- Separate customer payment records from technician payout records.
- Track refunds and credits carefully.
- Avoid expanding fixed costs before demand is proven.
- Review burn rate monthly.

## 11. Technician Payout Planning

Technician payouts should be:

- Clear before job assignment.
- Linked to completed and verified work.
- Paid on a predictable schedule.
- Adjusted only through documented rules.
- Protected from arbitrary deductions.

A fair payout model improves technician reliability and reduces off-platform behavior.

## 12. Pricing and Margin Governance

Every service category should have target margin ranges. Low-margin services may still be offered if they support customer acquisition, retention, or cross-sell, but the reason must be intentional.

## 13. Scenario Planning

FixNest should maintain three scenarios:

- Conservative case with slower bookings and higher acquisition cost.
- Base case with steady growth and stable margins.
- Upside case with strong repeat usage and successful service expansion.

Each scenario should include monthly revenue, jobs, gross margin, operating expenses, burn, and runway.

## 14. Funding Strategy

FixNest should initially focus on lean validation. External funding should be considered when the business can show:

- Reliable demand.
- Strong customer satisfaction.
- Repeat usage.
- Controlled complaint rate.
- Positive or improving unit economics.
- Scalable technician onboarding.

## 15. Financial Control Checklist

Before scaling, FixNest should have:

- Pricing master sheet.
- Technician payout tracker.
- Invoice records.
- Cash reconciliation process.
- Refund log.
- Monthly profit and loss summary.
- Unit economics report.
- Marketing spend report.
- Runway forecast.
